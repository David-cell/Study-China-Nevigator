How to Write a Study Plan for Chinese University Admission

The statement every degree applicant needs — and how to do it well.

Why it matters

Most Chinese universities require a study plan (or research proposal for graduate applicants) as a core document. It shows the admission committee your motivation, preparation and fit — and a weak one can sink an otherwise strong file.

Suggested structure

  1. Introduction & background: who you are, your current study, and why China.
  2. Why this school & program: specific reasons — faculty, lab, city, or a program that fits you. Show you have researched it.
  3. Academic & career goals: what you want to learn and where it leads.
  4. Research interests (graduate): the questions or methods you want to explore, and any advisor or group you hope to join.
  5. Language plan: how you will meet HSK/English requirements and improve.
  6. Conclusion: a brief, confident close tying it together.

Practical tips

  • Length: roughly 800 to 1,500 words, clear and specific.
  • Tailor each version to the school — do not send the same generic text everywhere.
  • Be honest and original; plagiarism is checked and penalised.
  • Link your plan to a CSC application if you are also applying for funding.
